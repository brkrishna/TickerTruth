India Backtest Integrity Report

Five NSE data errors that silently distort backtests, plus a pandas snippet to check each one against your own price series. No signup required.

1

Symbol discontinuity at merger or rename

When a company merges or renames, most vendors either drop the old ticker or start a new series with no link back to it. A strategy backtested against the new series alone never "traded" the pre-merger entity, even though it existed and had a price history.

Check: does your universe have a gap where a symbol should continue under a new name?

# Flag symbols whose price series ends abruptly with no delisting record
last_seen = df.groupby("symbol")["date"].max()
stale = last_seen[last_seen < df["date"].max() - pd.Timedelta(days=30)]
print(stale)  # investigate each: merger/rename, or a real gap?
2

Bonus issue mislabeled as a stock split

A 1:1 bonus and a 2:1 split both double the share count, but the adjustment math differs (a bonus doesn't reduce reserves the same way a split does). Vendors that normalize both to a generic "split factor" produce a price series that's off by the mismatch.

# Compare pre/post-event share count ratio against the vendor's recorded adjustment factor
implied_factor = new_shares / old_shares
mismatch = df[(df["action_type"] == "BONUS") &
              (df["adjustment_factor"] != implied_factor)]
print(mismatch)
3

Survivorship bias from dropped delistings

If your data feed only carries currently-listed securities, every company that delisted, went to zero, or was struck off is invisible to your backtest. That inflates Sharpe and CAGR on any strategy that would have held losers.

# Rough check: what fraction of your universe five years ago is still listed today?
listed_5y_ago = universe_snapshot("5y_ago")
still_listed = set(listed_5y_ago) & set(current_universe)
survivorship_ratio = len(still_listed) / len(listed_5y_ago)
print(f"{survivorship_ratio:.1%} of the 5-year-ago universe is still in your feed")
4

Announcement date vs ex-date misalignment

Event studies often key off the wrong date — using the announcement date when the price impact actually lands on the ex-date, or vice versa. For many NSE corporate action types the gap between the two is 1–3 weeks, enough to shift an entire cumulative abnormal return (CAR) window.

# Check the average gap between announcement and ex-date by action type
gap_days = (df["ex_date"] - df["announced_date"]).dt.days
print(df.groupby("action_type")["gap_days"].mean() # uses gap_days above)
5

Face-value change not tracked as a price adjustment

A face-value split (e.g. ₹10 → ₹2 per share) is economically a split, but some vendors treat it as a metadata-only change and leave the price series unadjusted — the series shows a sudden multi-fold drop that looks like a crash.

# Flag large single-day price drops that coincide with a face-value change record
pct_change = df.groupby("symbol")["close"].pct_change()
suspect = df[(pct_change < -0.4) &
             (df["symbol"].isin(face_value_change_symbols))]
print(suspect)
